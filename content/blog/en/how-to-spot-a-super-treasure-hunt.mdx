A Super Treasure Hunt, or STH, is a mainline car made in a special version and slipped into a few cases. On the pegs it sits among dozens of regular cars, often next to its own regular twin. These are the signs collectors use, from the quickest to the most certain.

## 1. Spectraflame paint

The first thing that catches the eye. Spectraflame is a translucent paint over polished metal, so the car looks deeper and shinier than the regular version, a bit like candy paint. The regular twin uses ordinary enamel, often in a close color. Under store lights, tilt the card: Spectraflame changes from light to dark much more than normal paint.

## 2. Real Riders rubber tires

Every Super has Real Riders: rubber tires on a two-piece wheel. Regular mainline cars have one-piece plastic wheels. Through the blister, rubber looks matte and slightly soft at the edges, plastic looks shiny and hard. This is the most reliable sign you can see without touching the car.

## 3. The gold flame on the card

On recent cards, a gold circle-flame logo is printed on the card art, behind the car. Collector sources disagree on whether it started in 2015 or 2016. Earlier cards look like regular ones, and from 2007 to 2011 the card said TREA$URE HUNT$ instead. A silver flame means a regular Treasure Hunt, not a Super: see [Treasure Hunt vs Super Treasure Hunt](/en/blog/treasure-hunt-vs-super-treasure-hunt).

## 4. The TH mark on the car

Since 2012, a small TH logo hides somewhere in the car's graphics: on a door, a fender, a license plate. It is easy to miss, so look for it once paint and tires already point to a Super.

## 5. The toy number on the back

This is the proof. Since 2013, every Super has its own toy number, different from its regular twin, printed on the back of the card. One year's Treasure Hunts and Supers share a code block: HKL in 2023, HTF in 2024, JBC in 2025, JJM in 2026. The 2026 Drift-Ender Super is JJM15, for example, while the regular Drift-Ender is JJH90. The collector number on the front (like 1/250) stays the same as the twin's, so it does not tell them apart.

## How to check a whole year quickly

Keep the list of the year in your pocket. The [2026 Super Treasure Hunt list](/en/super-treasure-hunt/2026) gives each car with its case, color and toy number, and the [2027 list](/en/super-treasure-hunt/2027) grows as new cases are revealed. If you know which case letter just reached your store, the [case tracker](/en/cases/2026) shows the one Super to look for.

## Traps to avoid

- **Loose cars.** Without the card, paint and tires can be faked. A loose car is a likely Super at best.
- **Resealed blisters.** A car swapped into an opened card cannot be seen in a photo. Check that the blister edges are clean and even.
- **Look-alike premium cars.** Car Culture and other premium lines also use rubber tires and metallic paint. A Super is always on a mainline card.
