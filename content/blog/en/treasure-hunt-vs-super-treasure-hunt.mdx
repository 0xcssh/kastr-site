Every year, two kinds of hunted cars hide among the Hot Wheels mainline: the Treasure Hunt (TH) and the Super Treasure Hunt (STH). They share a name, a flame logo and the same cases, which is why they get mixed up. Here is what sets them apart.

## A short history

The Treasure Hunt series started in 1995. Until 2006, every Treasure Hunt was a limited special car on its own card, often with rubber tires. In 2007 Hot Wheels split the idea in two: regular Treasure Hunts, closer to normal mainline cars, and Super Treasure Hunts, the special versions with premium paint and rubber tires. That split still holds today.

## Side by side

- **Paint.** TH: normal paint, often a color you would not expect. STH: Spectraflame, a translucent metallic finish over polished metal.
- **Wheels.** TH: plastic wheels, like any mainline car. STH: Real Riders, rubber tires on two-piece wheels.
- **Card logo.** TH: a silver circle-flame logo behind the car, since 2013. STH: a gold circle-flame logo, since 2015 or 2016 depending on the source.
- **Mark on the car.** TH: the flame logo in the graphics. STH: a small TH logo in the graphics.
- **Numbers.** TH: its own toy number and, according to the wiki's yearly lists, its own collector number. STH: its own toy number, but the same collector number as its regular twin.
- **How many.** Both run at roughly fifteen to twenty a year since 2013, one TH and one STH for each case letter from A to Q.

## Why the Super matters more to collectors

A regular Treasure Hunt is a nice find. A Super is the one people talk about: it is a premium car hidden at mainline price, and far fewer of them reach the stores. That is why the hunt lists on Kastr show the Supers first, with what sets each one apart from its regular twin.
