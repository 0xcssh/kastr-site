Hot Wheels collectors talk in shorthand. Here is what the words mean, in plain language. Words are grouped by theme; links lead to the longer guides.

## The lines

- **Mainline.** The basic cars sold one at a time in stores for a dollar or two. About 250 numbers a year.
- **Premium.** Higher-priced lines with metal bases, rubber tires and detailed graphics: Car Culture, Boulevard, Pop Culture, Team Transport and others.
- **Treasure Hunt (TH).** A mainline car marked as a hunt and packed into some cases, since 1995. Regular paint and wheels, a silver flame logo on recent cards.
- **Super Treasure Hunt (STH).** The rare version of a mainline car, with Spectraflame paint and Real Riders tires, since 2007. See [how to spot a Super Treasure Hunt](/en/blog/how-to-spot-a-super-treasure-hunt) and [Treasure Hunt vs Super Treasure Hunt](/en/blog/treasure-hunt-vs-super-treasure-hunt).
- **Chase.** In premium lines, a rarer version of one of the cars, packed into some cases. It often stands out by a ZAMAC body or a different finish.
- **Red Line Club (RLC).** Mattel's paid collector club. Members can buy limited cars online, often in Spectraflame with Real Riders, sometimes numbered.
- **Redline.** The original Hot Wheels made from 1968 to 1977, named after the red stripe on their tires. The most sought after vintage cars.

## Paint, wheels and metal

- **Spectraflame.** A translucent colored paint over a polished body, which gives a deep candy-like shine. Used on the first Redlines, and today on Supers and club cars.
- **Real Riders.** Rubber tires on a two-piece wheel, first used in 1983. Every Super has them; regular mainline cars have one-piece plastic wheels.
- **ZAMAC.** The zinc alloy the bodies are cast in. A ZAMAC car is left unpainted to show the bare metal, and some have been sold as store exclusives.
- **Metalflake.** Metallic paint with visible flakes, common on mainline cars. Not the same thing as Spectraflame.
- **Tampo.** The printed graphics on a car: stripes, logos, numbers.

## The car and its card

- **Casting.** The model itself, the shape of the body mold. One casting can come out in dozens of versions over the years.
- **Version (or variation).** One release of a casting: a given year, color, wheels and graphics.
- **Toy number.** The code on the back of the card, like JJM18, that names one version. The most precise way to search for a car: try it in the [value checker](/en/value).
- **Collector number.** The number on the front of the card, like 34/250, the car's place in the year's mainline. A Super shares it with its regular twin.
- **Series.** The theme a mainline car belongs to that year, like HW Dirt, with its own count (3/10).
- **Case.** A factory carton of 72 mainline cars. Each year has about fifteen, lettered A to Q without I and O. See [case codes explained](/en/blog/hot-wheels-case-codes-explained).
- **Carded.** Still in its package, on its card under a plastic blister. The opposite is **loose**.
- **Base code.** A short code stamped on the base of the car that dates its production.

## Hunting slang

- **Pegs.** The hooks where the cars hang in stores. "Hitting the pegs" means checking stores.
- **Peg warmer.** A car nobody buys, which stays on the pegs for months.
- **Scalper.** Someone who clears the pegs to resell. Most collectors frown on it.
- **Double.** A car you own twice, often by mistake. Kastr flags the cars you already own before you buy.
- **Grail.** The car a collector wants most.
