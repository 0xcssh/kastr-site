A collection you cannot search is a box of cars. Cataloging it takes an evening the first time, then a few seconds per new car, and it pays back the first time you do not buy a car you already own.

## What to record for each car

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Toy number | The code on the back of the card, like JJM18. It names one exact version, so everything else can be filled in from it |
| Name and year | To read your list at a glance |
| Condition | Carded or loose, and the state of the card |
| Price paid | To know what the collection cost you |
| Where and when | Store, date: useful for your hunt log |
| Quantity | Two copies on purpose are not a mistake |
| Notes and photo | Errors, signatures, the details only you know |

The toy number is the field that saves the most time: with it, a name like "Twin Mill" stops being ambiguous among dozens of versions. Look it up in the [value checker](/en/value) if you are unsure.

## Starting with a spreadsheet

For a few dozen cars, a spreadsheet does the job. One row per car, one column per field above, the toy number first. Sort by year or by series to spot gaps.

It gets hard past a hundred or so cars: you type every detail by hand, nothing checks your entries, and the sheet is rarely with you in the store when you need it.

## Moving to an app

An app that knows the catalog does the typing for you. In Kastr:

- **Scan or search.** Point the camera at the card, or type a name or toy number: the exact version comes up with its year, series and color.
- **Already in the garage.** Every car you look at shows whether you own it, even offline.
- **Value.** Each car shows its value, and the garage its total against what you paid.
- **Several garages.** Keep, say, carded cars and loose ones apart (with Pro).

## Importing what you already have

You do not have to retype a collection. Kastr imports a CSV or Excel file, a tab-separated export from Numbers or Google Sheets, or the export of another app:

1. Export your sheet as CSV or keep it as an Excel file.
2. Import it in the app: it guesses which column holds the toy number, the name, the year, the price and the date.
3. Each line is matched to the catalog, by toy number first, then by name and year.
4. When a line fits several versions you choose the right one, and the report counts the lines it could not match, so nothing disappears silently.

## Keep an exit

Whatever tool you use, export your collection from time to time. A CSV file opens anywhere and moves to any other app. Kastr exports the whole garage as CSV, and as a PDF report with photos for Pro members.
